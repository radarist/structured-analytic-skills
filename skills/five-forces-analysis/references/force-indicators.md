# Five Forces — indicator checklists

Companion reference for `five-forces-analysis`. Indicators are drawn from M. E. Porter,
"The Five Competitive Forces That Shape Strategy," *Harvard Business Review*, vol. 86,
no. 1, pp. 78–93, Jan. 2008, and *Competitive Strategy* (Free Press, 1980). Rate each
force Low / Medium / High and cite evidence per indicator; three or more indicators per
force is the minimum for a defensible rating.

## Force 1 — Threat of new entrants

Porter names seven sources of entry barriers: supply-side economies of scale; demand-side
benefits of scale (network effects); customer switching costs; capital requirements;
incumbency advantages independent of size (proprietary technology, favoured locations,
established brand, cumulative experience); unequal access to distribution channels; and
restrictive government policy. Expected retaliation matters as much as the barriers: it is
the *threat* of entry, not actual entry, that holds down profitability.

- High barriers / low threat: large minimum efficient scale, strong network effects, high
  switching costs, heavy capital requirements, locked distribution, licensing regimes.
- Low barriers / high threat: commodified inputs, no scale advantage, trivial switching,
  open channels, no regulatory gate.

## Force 2 — Bargaining power of suppliers

Powerful when the supplier group is more concentrated than the industry it sells to; when
it does not depend heavily on the industry for revenue; when buyers face switching costs;
when suppliers offer differentiated products; when no substitute exists for what the
supplier group provides; and when suppliers can credibly threaten forward integration.
Labour is a supplier group — scarce specialist talent is supplier power.

## Force 3 — Bargaining power of buyers

Powerful when buyers are few or purchase in large volumes; when products are standardised
or undifferentiated; when switching costs are low; and when buyers can credibly threaten
backward integration. Price sensitivity is separate from leverage: buyers are price
sensitive when the purchase is a large share of their cost structure, when they earn low
profits, when quality is little affected by the product, and when the product has limited
effect on their own costs. Intermediate customers (channels) gain power when they
influence the end customer's purchasing decision.

## Force 4 — Threat of substitutes

A substitute performs the same or a similar function by a different means — video
conferencing for travel, plastic for aluminium, email for express mail. The threat is high
when the substitute offers an attractive price-performance trade-off relative to the
industry's product, and when the buyer's cost of switching to it is low. Watch for
substitution arriving from outside the industry, and for price-performance improvements in
the substitute that outrun the industry's own. Substitution can be indirect, where a
substitute displaces a buyer industry's product.

## Force 5 — Rivalry among existing competitors

Intensity is greatest when competitors are numerous or roughly equal in size and power;
industry growth is slow; exit barriers are high; rivals are highly committed and have
aspirations for leadership; and firms cannot read each other's signals well. The *basis* of
competition matters as much as intensity: price competition transfers profit straight to
buyers and is most damaging when products are near-identical, fixed costs are high and
marginal costs low, capacity must be added in large increments, or the product is
perishable. Competition on features, service, brand or lead time is more likely to be
positive-sum and can expand the industry.

## Factors that are not forces

Porter treats industry growth rate, technology and innovation, government, and
complementary products as **factors that act through the five forces**, not as a sixth
force. Their effect on profitability is determined by how they change entry barriers,
buyer or supplier power, substitution, or rivalry — a fast-growing industry with powerful
buyers and attractive substitutes is not profitable.
