# Backcasting — variants, participatory practice and common pathway failures

Supplement to `../SKILL.md`.

## Variants

| Variant | Fixed point | Typical use | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy/resource backcasting | A quantified end-state system description | Policy analysis of long-lived infrastructure | Robinson, *Energy Policy*, 1982; *Futures*, 1990 |
| Backcasting from principles | A set of sustainability principles rather than one picture | Firm and municipal strategy where the desirable end state is contested in detail but agreed in principle | Holmberg & Robèrt, 2000 |
| Participatory / stakeholder backcasting | A vision co-produced by stakeholders, with follow-up and diffusion as part of the method | Sustainability transitions, regional planning | Quist & Vergragt, 2006 |
| Multi-vision backcasting | Several competing visions backcast in parallel | Contested value space, no single mandate holder | Quist & Vergragt, 2006 |

Dreborg (1996) gives the selection rule: backcasting suits problems that are complex and affect many sectors, where a major change is needed, where dominant trends are part of the problem, where externalities are not resolved by markets, and where the time horizon is long enough for deliberate choice.

## What "backwards" means in practice

Each backward step asks: for milestone M dated year T, what must already exist in year T−n, given the lead time of the slowest enabling asset? The chain is therefore paced by physical and institutional lead times, not by planning convenience:

- asset replacement cycles (vehicles 8–15 years, boilers 15–25, buildings 30–60);
- grid connections and substation works (commonly 3–7 years);
- permitting and statutory consultation;
- training and accreditation pipelines for new skills;
- procurement frameworks and contract break points.

## Common pathway failures

- **The renamed present.** Early milestones are projects already funded. Test: remove the vision; would the first five years look different?
- **The miracle step.** A discontinuity with no precedent and no owner. Test: name the jurisdiction or firm that did something comparable, and how long it took.
- **The back-loaded chain.** Everything hard happens after the current leadership's horizon. Test: what fraction of the required change is scheduled in the first third of the period?
- **The single route.** One pathway, no decision point, no fallback. Test: what is the plan if the flagged technology slips two years?
- **The unowned vision.** No one is accountable for the definition of "desirable"; the backcast then encodes the analyst's values.
