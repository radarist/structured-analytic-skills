# STEEP / PESTLE Analysis

> **Essence:** A coverage checklist for the macro-environment — Social, Technological, Economic, Environmental, Political (+Legal/Ethical) — that forces analysts to look for change drivers in every category before deciding which ones actually matter.

## Overview

STEEP (and its siblings PEST, PESTLE, PESTEL, STEEPLED, STEER) is the simplest, most widely taught environmental-analysis framework. The analyst takes a question or strategy and systematically inventories external factors in each category: **Social** (demographics, values, lifestyles), **Technological** (innovation, infrastructure, R&D), **Economic** (growth, rates, employment, trade), **Environmental/Ecological** (climate, resources, pollution), **Political** (governance, policy, stability), sometimes split into **Legal** (regulation, law) and **Ethical/Demographic** additions (STEEPLED adds Ethical and Demographic).

Its function is discipline against tunnel vision: left alone, strategists analyze the categories they already understand (usually market and technology) and ignore the rest. The framework itself is trivially simple — the value is in (a) completeness of the sweep, (b) *rating* factors for impact and uncertainty rather than listing them, and (c) using the rated output as the raw material for real analysis: scenario axes ([scenario-planning.md](scenario-planning.md)), risk registers, opportunity screens.

On its own, an unprocessed PESTLE list is decoration. Rated, clustered, and connected, it is the standard bridge from [horizon-scanning.md](horizon-scanning.md) to scenario work.

## Origin & History

- Rooted in **Francis Aguilar's** environmental scanning (*Scanning the Business Environment*, 1967), which used the taxonomy ETPS (Economic, Technical, Political, Social) — later permuted by generations of strategy textbooks into PEST.
- Through the 1980s–90s the acronym accreted letters in strategy and marketing practice: **PEST → PESTLE/PESTEL** (adding Legal and Environmental) and **STEEP** (reordered, common in futures work) and **STEEPLED** (+Ethical, +Demographic). No single inventor; it is professional-practice folklore codified in texts like Johnson, Whittington & Scholes, *Exploring Strategy*.
- In foresight practice, STEEP became the default coverage frame for driving-force identification in scenario planning (e.g., in the GBN/Schwartz process and UK Foresight practice).

## Core Concepts & Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Factor | A specific, current-or-emerging external condition (e.g., "aging population in the EU"), not a vague category label. |
| Driver | A factor with momentum that will shape the future context; the foresight-relevant subset of factors. |
| Impact rating | Judged magnitude of effect on the focal question (typically 1–5). |
| Uncertainty rating | Judged unpredictability of the factor's future state (typically 1–5). |
| Predetermined element | High-impact, low-uncertainty factor — goes into all scenarios/plans (Schwartz's term). |
| Critical uncertainty | High-impact, high-uncertainty factor — scenario-axis candidate. |
| Category coverage check | Auditing that no STEEP letter was skipped or trivially filled. |

## When to Use / When Not to Use

**Use when:**
- Starting any strategic analysis: a fast completeness sweep of the macro-environment.
- Converting scanning output into a structured factor inventory for scenarios, risk, or planning.
- Workshops with mixed backgrounds — the categories give everyone an entry point.
- As a periodic audit ("which category have we stopped watching?").

**Don't use when:**
- You need dynamics, causality, or system structure (it is a checklist, not a model — use causal-loop mapping or [cross-impact-analysis.md](cross-impact-analysis.md)).
- The analysis must prioritize — an unrated PESTLE list prioritizes nothing; always add impact × uncertainty scoring.
- The environment is dominated by 1–2 known forces (go straight to analyzing them).
- As a deliverable to impress: a giant unprioritized list signals unfinished thinking.

## Process & Steps

1. **Define the focal question and scope.** What decision, market, geography, and horizon does this analysis serve? *Artifact: framing statement.*
2. **Inventory factors per category.** Per letter, list concrete, specific factors (with evidence where possible — feed from [horizon-scanning.md](horizon-scanning.md)). Aim for specificity: "EU AI Act enforcement practice crystallizing 2025–27", not "regulation". *Artifact: factor inventory (20–60 items).*
3. **Rate each factor.** Impact on the focal question (1–5) and uncertainty about its future state (1–5), with a one-line rationale. *Artifact: rated factor table.*
4. **Cluster and name drivers.** Group related factors into a smaller set of named driving forces (8–15). *Artifact: driver list.*
5. **Sort by the 2×2 (impact × uncertainty).** Predetermined elements (high impact, low uncertainty) → planning assumptions. Critical uncertainties (high/high) → scenario-axis candidates or watchlist. Low-impact factors → drop. *Artifact: impact/uncertainty map.*
6. **Hand off.** Feed drivers into [scenario-planning.md](scenario-planning.md), risk registers, or strategy options; archive the inventory for the next cycle. *Artifact: driver handoff pack.*

**Timing:** solo, 1–2 days; workshop, half a day for steps 2–5 with 6–15 participants.

## Techniques, Tools & Deliverables

- Impact × uncertainty scatter/matrix (the standard visual).
- Inter-factor linkage notes (factors rarely act alone — mark strong couplings).
- Coverage audit table (factors per category; empty categories are findings, not successes).
- **Tools:** spreadsheet + whiteboard; nothing else required.
- **Deliverables:** rated factor inventory, driver list, impact/uncertainty map, handoff pack.

## Strengths & Limitations

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Fast, cheap, universally understood | Static checklist — no dynamics or causality |
| Guards against category blindness | Invites laundry lists unless ratings are enforced |
| Perfect front-end for scenario work | Category boundaries are fuzzy (factors fit several letters) |
| Accessible to any workshop participant | False comfort: completeness of categories ≠ completeness of insight |
| Easy to automate partially | Category names bias toward macro, away from industry-specific forces |

The trap is false completeness: six filled columns feel like coverage, but the categories only discipline *breadth across the macro environment* — they say nothing about depth, and they actively crowd out the industry-specific forces that usually matter more. The laundry-list failure is the common one in practice: an unrated PESTLE list is decoration, and even a rated one is only as good as the evidence behind the ratings. Treat STEEP as a coverage checklist whose output is inputs (rated drivers, critical uncertainties) for trend analysis and scenarios — never as an analysis in itself.

## Worked Examples & Case Studies

- **Standard practice in scenario exercises:** the GBN/Schwartz 8-step process uses environmental categories at its step 3 (driving forces); UK Foresight projects routinely present STEEP-framed driver inventories as the bridge from evidence to scenarios — e.g. *Tackling Obesities: Future Choices* (2007), where the driver sweep fed the system map and the scenarios that shaped UK obesity policy debate.
- **Corporate strategy offsites:** PESTLE tables are the default opening exercise in countless strategic planning processes (documented across strategy textbooks, e.g., Johnson, Whittington & Scholes).
- **Policy appraisal:** UK and EU policy units use PESTLE-style checklists in options analysis and regulatory scanning.

## Variants & Related Methodologies

- **PEST, PESTEL, STEEP, STEEPLED, STEER, SLEPT, LONGPESTLE** — same idea, different letter budgets.
- **SWOT** — pairs external factors (O/T) with internal ones (S/W); often fed by a PESTLE.
- **Issue-specific checklists** — e.g., regulatory-only deep dives.

## Agent Adaptation

### Suitability for agent execution

**High.** Factor inventory, evidence attachment, deduplication, clustering, and even first-pass impact/uncertainty rating are well within agent capability — this is essentially a structured research-and-classification task. The ratings that matter (what is truly critical *for us*) involve organizational context and risk appetite, so final ratings and axis selection stay with humans or get human sign-off.

### Recommended multi-agent workflow

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Scout (×6, one per STEEP letter) | Research factors in its category with sources. |
| Analyst | Merge, dedupe, cluster into drivers; draft impact/uncertainty ratings with rationale. |
| Critic / Red Team | Challenge vague factors ("regulation"), missing couplings, category stuffing; demand specificity. |
| Domain Expert (personas) | Re-rate the top drivers from the organization's actual strategic position. |
| Synthesizer | Produce the rated inventory, impact/uncertainty map, and handoff pack. |
| Verifier | Confirm factor claims and dates against sources. |
| Facilitator (human) | Sets focal question; approves final driver ratings. |

### Agent pipeline

1. Frame (human) → focal question, geography, horizon.
2. Gather (6 parallel Scouts) → `factors_<letter>.json` [factor, evidence, source_urls].
3. Analyze (Analyst) → merged, deduped, clustered `drivers.csv` [driver, categories, impact, uncertainty, rationale, member_factors].
4. Stress-test (Critic) → specificity and coverage challenges → revised drivers.
5. Verify (Verifier) → source checks.
6. Report (Synthesizer) → rated inventory + 2×2 map → human rating review → handoff to scenario planning.

### Prompt templates

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Scout for the {{steep_letter}} category of a STEEP analysis. Focal question:
"{{focal_question}}". Geography: {{geography}}. Horizon: {{horizon}} years. Identify 8-15 concrete,
current or emerging factors in this category that could materially affect the question. Each
factor must be specific (named law, measured trend, identifiable development), carry one piece of
evidence and a source URL. Reject generic entries ("technology is advancing"). JSON output.
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Analyst. Here are the factor lists for all STEEP categories: {{factors_json}}.
(1) Deduplicate and merge overlapping factors. (2) Cluster into 8-15 named driving forces.
(3) Rate each driver: impact on the focal question 1-5, uncertainty about its future state 1-5,
one-line rationale each. (4) Mark strong couplings between drivers. Output CSV:
driver,categories,impact,uncertainty,rationale,coupled_with. Focal question: "{{focal_question}}".
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Critic. Review this STEEP driver table: {{drivers_csv}}. Attack: (a) factors
stated as vague nouns rather than concrete developments; (b) categories with thin coverage — what
is missing? (c) ratings that look unconsidered (everything 4/4?); (d) drivers that are actually
internal, not external. Return a numbered list of required fixes.
```

### Tools & data requirements

Web search (Scout/Verifier), spreadsheet/JSON store, retrieval over organizational strategy documents for context-aware rating.

### Quality checks, failure modes & mitigations

| Failure mode | Detection | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Laundry list (60 unrated factors) | No ratings present | Pipeline gate: unrated factors cannot ship |
| Vague factor names | Critic pass | Specificity rule: every factor must be evidence-attachable |
| Category stuffing (empty E, thin L) | Coverage audit table | Report coverage explicitly; thin categories trigger a re-sweep |
| Ratings mirror generic priors, not this organization | Ratings identical across focal questions | Domain Expert re-rate with org context; human sign-off |
| Stale inventory reused for years | Factor dates old | Timestamp every factor; expire on cadence |

### Human-in-the-loop checkpoints

1. Focal question and scope.
2. Final driver ratings and axis selection (feeds scenarios).
3. Deciding which critical uncertainties get monitoring.

### Inputs & outputs (chaining contract)

**Inputs:** focal question; scanning output (from [horizon-scanning.md](horizon-scanning.md)); trend data (from [trend-analysis.md](trend-analysis.md)).
**Outputs:** rated driver inventory, impact/uncertainty map — feeding [scenario-planning.md](scenario-planning.md) (axis candidates), risk registers, strategy processes.
