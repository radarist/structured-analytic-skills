# Causal Layered Analysis (CLA)

> **Essence:** A critical-futures method that analyzes an issue at four depths — the surface litany, systemic causes, competing worldviews, and deep myths/metaphors — so that futures work transforms meaning, not just trends.

## Overview

Most futures methods operate on the surface of an issue: the data, the trends, the visible system. Causal Layered Analysis, developed by Sohail Inayatullah, insists that how a problem is *constituted* — the worldview that frames it and the deep stories that make the frame feel natural — determines which futures can even be imagined. CLA therefore deconstructs an issue through four layers:

1. **The Litany** — the visible, headline level: quantitative trends, events, media noise ("crime is rising", "AI will take jobs"). Solutions here are reactive and short-term; the framing is rarely questioned.
2. **Systemic Causes** — the social, economic, political, and technological structures producing the litany (incentives, institutions, feedback loops). Solutions here are policy-level.
3. **Worldview / Discourse** — the deeper paradigm: which ways of knowing and valuing make this system seem sensible? Competing worldviews produce different definitions of "the problem" itself (growth paradigm vs degrowth paradigm; security discourse vs care discourse).
4. **Myth / Metaphor** — the deepest layer: archetypal stories, images, and metaphors that carry the issue emotionally ("progress", "the fortress", "the machine", "the flood"). These operate below argument and are the hardest to change — and the most powerful to change.

CLA is used in two movements. **Deconstruction**: move down the layers of an existing issue to expose how it is constructed. **Reconstruction**: build an alternative future by deliberately choosing a new myth/metaphor, a worldview coherent with it, redesigned systems, and a different resulting litany — the layers must be vertically coherent or the new future will collapse back into the old story.

CLA is the principal method of **critical futures studies**: it treats futures work as intervention in meaning, and it pairs naturally with [scenario-planning.md](scenario-planning.md) (CLA deepens scenarios; scenarios give CLA concrete worlds) and with Inayatullah's other tools (see [futures-triangle.md](futures-triangle.md)).

## Origin & History

- Developed by **Sohail Inayatullah** (Pakistani-Australian futurist, Tamkang University / University of the Sunshine Coast) in the early 1990s, drawing on poststructuralism (Foucault's discourse), critical theory, and depth psychology/myth (also influenced by his work with Ivana Milojević).
- Landmark statement: Inayatullah, S. (1998). "Causal Layered Analysis: Poststructuralism as Method." *Futures*, 30(8).
- Elaborated in Inayatullah (ed.), *The Causal Layered Analysis (CLA) Reader* (2004), and applied across government foresight (notably in Australia, and in UN-context futures work) and organizational strategy; standard content in futures-studies curricula.

## Core Concepts & Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Litany | Layer 1: surface trends, headlines, quantitative noise; the issue as conventionally presented. |
| Systemic causes | Layer 2: structures, institutions, incentives, technologies producing the litany. |
| Worldview / discourse | Layer 3: the paradigm and discourse that legitimize the system; defines what counts as the problem and as evidence. |
| Myth / metaphor | Layer 4: deep archetypal stories and images carrying the issue emotionally. |
| Deconstruction | Moving down the layers to expose construction and contingency. |
| Reconstruction | Building a vertically coherent alternative across all four layers. |
| Vertical coherence | Requirement that myth → worldview → system → litany mutually reinforce in a reconstructed future. |
| CLA of the future vs of the present | Applying the layers to a preferred future (design) vs to the current issue (critique). |

## When to Use / When Not to Use

**Use when:**
- The issue is stuck: decades of policy and the problem keeps reproducing (obesity, crime, congestion) — a sign the framing itself is the problem.
- Futures work keeps producing "more of the same" scenarios; you need genuinely alternative imaginaries.
- Stakeholders talk past each other — often a worldview-layer conflict, not a data conflict.
- Deepening scenarios or visions with cultural/discursive texture.
- Research in critical futures, cultural change, communication strategy.

**Don't use when:**
- The problem is technical and well-framed (engineering optimization, logistics) — CLA overhead buys nothing.
- The group wants forecasts or roadmaps this quarter — CLA is slow, reflective, and unsettling.
- Facilitation skill is unavailable: layer 3–4 work requires comfort with ambiguity and conflict.
- The output must be quantified or directly decision-grade.

## Process & Steps

Typical workshop design (half day to 2 days; also a research-analytic method over weeks):

1. **Frame the issue.** One concrete issue statement as currently discussed (write down the conventional litany framing — you'll return to it). *Artifact: issue statement.*
2. **Map the Litany.** How is the issue presented in media/data/official talk? List the headline facts and stock phrases. *Artifact: litany inventory.*
3. **Map Systemic Causes.** What structures produce this? Incentives, institutions, technologies, demographics; include how the current system *reproduces* the problem. *Artifact: systemic map.*
4. **Map Worldviews.** Which paradigm makes this system sensible? Whose discourse is it (experts? markets? security?)? Articulate 1–2 *competing* worldviews explicitly. *Artifact: worldview statements.*
5. **Map Myths/Metaphors.** What deep stories carry it? ("Growth is life", "the war on X", "the flood of migrants", "the machine that must be fed".) *Artifact: myth/metaphor list.*
6. **Deconstruct.** Where is the conventional framing contingent? What does it hide, whom does it serve, what does it make unthinkable? *Artifact: deconstruction notes.*
7. **Reconstruct an alternative.** Choose a different myth/metaphor for the preferred future; derive the worldview coherent with it; redesign systems/policies; describe the resulting new litany. Check vertical coherence. *Artifact: four-layer alternative future table.*
8. **Integrate.** Use the reconstructed layers to enrich scenarios, vision statements, or strategy narratives; identify the communication shifts (new metaphors) that carry the change. *Artifact: CLA-informed futures outputs.*

## Techniques, Tools & Deliverables

- The four-layer table (rows = layers, columns = current issue / alternative future) — the canonical artifact.
- "Headline harvesting" from media for the litany layer.
- Metaphor elicitation: "complete the sentence: this issue is like a ___".
- Scenario deepening: write one scenario per worldview-layer position.
- **Deliverables:** completed CLA table, deconstruction notes, reconstructed future narrative, communication/metaphor recommendations.

## Strengths & Limitations

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Reaches where other methods can't: meaning, identity, story | Requires skilled facilitation; layer 3–4 work is genuinely hard |
| Explains why problems reproduce despite policy | Resists quantification and audit trails |
| Produces truly alternative futures, not trend extrapolations | Can be received as ideological critique (it *is* critical) — political risk in client settings |
| Vertical coherence test is a powerful design discipline | Outcomes are narratives and framings — indirect path to decisions |
| Integrates well with scenarios and visioning | Evidence standards for myth/worldview claims are interpretive, not empirical |

The honest caveat: CLA's deepest layers are its least verifiable. A litany critique can be checked against data; a "myth/metaphor" reading cannot — it persuades or it doesn't. In client settings this cuts both ways: the method has surfaced real blind spots (problems that reproduce because the shared story forbids the solution), and it has also produced sessions where layer-4 work degenerated into evocative but unactionable language. It fails outright when the client needs an auditable decision trail, when the room has no psychological safety for worldview talk, or when the facilitator can't distinguish a genuine deep metaphor from a clever-sounding one. Budget the political cost: naming an organization's founding myth is a critical act, and some rooms will hear it as an attack.

## Worked Examples & Case Studies

- **Inayatullah's published cases:** applications to cities ("the city as machine" vs "city as garden/organism"), crime (from "war on crime" litany to community/health metaphors), and Asia-Pacific futures (documented across the *CLA Reader* and *Futures* journal).
- **Health/obesity policy CLA:** reframing obesity from individual-failure litany through food-system structure to worldview (consumer choice paradigm) and myth ("the disciplined body") — used in public-health futures teaching and research.
- **Government foresight integration:** used in Australian and international government futures projects to deepen scenario and policy work (documented in the futures literature).

## Variants & Related Methodologies

- **CLA of the future (design mode)** — start at myth for a preferred future and build up.
- **Nested/integrated CLA + scenarios** — CLA table per scenario (Inayatullah's "alternative futures + CLA" practice).
- **CLA 2.0 / extensions** — additions like an embodied/spiritual layer in some practice.

## Agent Adaptation

### Suitability for agent execution

**Medium.** Agents are strong at the mechanical parts: harvesting litanies from media, mapping systemic causes, generating candidate metaphors, drafting reconstructed futures, and — especially — checking vertical coherence across layers. The worldview layer needs adversarial diversity: a single agent will default to one discourse, so persona-based competing worldviews are essential. But the method's deepest value is a *group's* encounter with its own myths; agent-generated tables can prepare and enrich that encounter, not replace it. Also note: myth/metaphor claims are interpretive — the Verifier role applies to factual litany/system claims, not to layer 3–4 content.

### Recommended multi-agent workflow

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Scout | Harvest the litany: headlines, stock phrases, official statistics, and their framings (with sources). |
| Analyst | Draft systemic-cause map; assemble the four-layer table; check vertical coherence. |
| Domain Expert (×3+ personas with *genuinely different worldviews*) | Articulate competing worldview-layer readings of the issue. |
| Critic / Red Team | Challenge: is layer 3 actually contested or a single discourse? Are proposed metaphors alive in the culture or invented? Is the reconstruction coherent or slogan-stacking? |
| Synthesizer | Produce the CLA table, deconstruction notes, reconstructed narrative, metaphor recommendations. |
| Verifier | Source-check litany/statistics/system claims only. |
| Facilitator (human) | Owns the issue framing and approves the reconstructed future (values choice). |

### Agent pipeline

1. Frame (human) → issue statement.
2. Gather (Scout) → `litany.json` [phrase/claim, source_url, framing_notes].
3. Analyze (Analyst) → `cla_table_draft.md` (four layers); Domain Experts supply competing worldview columns.
4. Stress-test (Critic) → discourse-diversity check, metaphor-liveness check, coherence check.
5. Verify (Verifier) → empirical claims in layers 1–2.
6. Human session → debate worldviews, choose reconstruction direction.
7. Report (Synthesizer) → final CLA table + reconstructed narrative → feeds scenarios/vision work.

### Prompt templates

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Scout for a Causal Layered Analysis of "{{issue}}". Harvest the LITANY: how
this issue is actually talked about. Collect 15-25 items: headline claims, stock phrases, iconic
statistics, official-communication framings — each with source URL and a one-line note on the
framing (fear? technocratic? moral panic? progress?). Include framings from at least 3 different
media/political positions. JSON output.
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are a worldview analyst in a CLA exercise on "{{issue}}". Your persona embodies the
{{worldview}} worldview (e.g., market-liberal / ecological / security / care / indigenous /
techno-optimist). From YOUR paradigm: What IS the problem here (it differs by worldview)? What
causes it? What solutions are thinkable — and what is unthinkable — in your frame? What deep
story or metaphor carries your position? Be authentically this worldview, not a parody of it.
400-600 words.
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Critic reviewing this CLA table: {{cla_table}}. Checks: (1) Are the worldview
entries genuinely different discourses, or one discourse with adjectives? (2) Are the
myth/metaphor entries ALIVE in the culture (findable in media, speech, ritual) or invented by
the analyst? (3) Vertical coherence of the reconstruction: would the proposed new systems
actually produce the new litany, and would the new myth sustain the new worldview — or does the
old myth eat the new system? (4) What does the whole analysis refuse to see? Structured output.
```

### Tools & data requirements

Web search (litany harvesting, metaphor-liveness checks); no quantitative tooling needed; a document store for source-cited claims.

### Quality checks, failure modes & mitigations

| Failure mode | Detection | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Single-discourse worldview layer | Critic check 1 | Persona-enforced competing worldviews; at least 3 columns |
| Invented metaphors | Critic check 2 | Metaphor-liveness rule: must be findable in real discourse |
| Slogan-stacked reconstruction | Critic check 3 | Coherence audit: each layer must imply the next |
| Analysis becomes the agents' own ideology | Human session finds nothing to debate | Present ≥2 reconstructions from different myths |
| Litany claims unverified | Verifier | Sources mandatory at layers 1–2 |

### Human-in-the-loop checkpoints

1. Issue framing.
2. The worldview debate (this is the method's heart).
3. Choosing the myth/metaphor for reconstruction — a values decision.
4. Deciding what the CLA output feeds (scenarios, communication, strategy).

### Inputs & outputs (chaining contract)

**Inputs:** an issue statement; media/discourse material (Scout can harvest; also from [horizon-scanning.md](horizon-scanning.md)).
**Outputs:** CLA table, reconstructed future narrative, metaphor recommendations — feeding [scenario-planning.md](scenario-planning.md) (deeper scenarios), [three-horizons.md](three-horizons.md) (H3 identity), [backcasting.md](backcasting.md) (vision depth), communication strategy.
