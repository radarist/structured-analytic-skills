# Three Horizons

> **Essence:** A sense-making framework that reads the present as three overlapping waves — the declining dominant system (H1), the disruptive transition zone (H2), and the emerging future (H3) — and turns conflict about change into a structured conversation about what to keep, what to seed, and how to manage the transition.

## Overview

Three Horizons maps change as three curves over time. **H1** is the current dominant way of doing things: still powerful, but its fit to the environment is declining and its curve eventually falls. **H3** is the emerging future: today visible only as "pockets of the future in the present" — fringe practices, weak signals, visionary experiments — but embodying a better fit with where the world is going. **H2** is the turbulent space of innovation between them, where two kinds of activity compete: **H2−** innovations that get captured by H1 and merely extend its life (sustaining innovations), and **H2+** innovations that carry transformation toward H3.

The method's power is conversational, not predictive. It gives incumbents (who defend H1), entrepreneurs (who build H2), and visionaries (who see H3) a shared map on which each can locate themselves and each other, and it reframes strategy as three simultaneous questions: What of H1 is dying and must be let go — and what is valuable and must be *conserved* into the future? What H3 seeds already exist and how do we nourish them? Which H2 innovations should we back, and how do we stop them being captured by H1?

Unlike scenario planning (multiple alternative futures), Three Horizons is committed to direction: it assumes systems transform, and works on *how*.

## Origin & History

- Developed in the mid-2000s by **Bill Sharpe and Anthony Hodgson** with colleagues at the **International Futures Forum (IFF)**; first widely cited paper: Curry, A. & Hodgson, A. (2008), "Seeing in Multiple Horizons: Connecting Futures to Strategy", *Journal of Futures Studies*, 13(1).
- Codified by **Bill Sharpe**, *Three Horizons: The Patterning of Hope* (2013), which frames the method as a practice of "future consciousness" with detailed facilitation guidance.
- Adopted broadly in public-service innovation (notably the UK's futures and policy-design community), education, health systems, sustainability transitions, and organizational strategy; related lineage to the "S-curve" family of technology-transition thinking.

## Core Concepts & Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| H1 (First Horizon) | The dominant current system: "managerial" voice, maintains and defends the status quo; its curve declines as environmental fit decays. |
| H2 (Second Horizon) | The transition zone of disruptive innovation: "entrepreneur" voice; ambiguous, fast-moving, contested. |
| H2+ | Transition innovations aligned with the H3 future (transformative). |
| H2− | Innovations captured by H1 that extend the old system (e.g., "greener" versions of a failing model). |
| H3 (Third Horizon) | The emerging future system: "visionary" voice; present today only in pockets and weak signals. |
| Pockets of the future in the present | Observable, real practices today that embody H3 (Sharpe's phrase; the empirical anchor of the method). |
| Pattern of renewal | The healthy dynamic: H1 declines gracefully, conserving its value; H2 experiments; H3 scales. |
| Dilemma / tension | Each horizon faces characteristic dilemmas (e.g., H1: keep serving today vs fund tomorrow). |
| Facilitative consciousness | The stance of holding all three voices in one conversation without letting H1 dominate. |

## When to Use / When Not to Use

**Use when:**
- A system (organization, sector, community) knows it must transform but is stuck in H1-vs-H3 shouting matches.
- You need to distinguish genuinely transformative innovation from life-support for the old model (H2+ vs H2−).
- Strategy conversations keep collapsing into either "defend the core" or "burn it down".
- As the bridge between scenarios/vision work and concrete innovation portfolios.

**Don't use when:**
- The question is which of several *alternative* futures might arrive (use [scenario-planning.md](scenario-planning.md)).
- You need quantified projections (use [trend-analysis.md](trend-analysis.md) or modeling).
- The group has no tolerance for ambiguity — H2 is explicitly messy.
- A purely technical substitution is at stake with no systemic dimension.

## Process & Steps

Typical facilitated workshop (1–2 days; can run distributed over weeks):

1. **Frame the domain.** Agree the system under discussion (e.g., "primary care in our region", "our media business"). *Artifact: domain definition.*
2. **Map H1 — the current system.** What dominates today? Which aspects are declining/failing (and why — loss of fit with the environment)? Crucially: what is *valuable* in H1 that must be conserved into the future? *Artifact: H1 curve annotated with decline signals + conservation list.*
3. **Map H3 — the emerging future.** What would a fundamentally better-fit system look like? Where do its "pockets of the future" already exist today — real, nameable examples? *Artifact: H3 vision themes + inventory of present-day pockets.*
4. **Map H2 — the transition zone.** What innovations are emerging? For each: does it extend H1 (H2−) or enable H3 (H2+)? *Artifact: H2 innovation map sorted by capture risk.*
5. **Hold the three-voice conversation.** Facilitate explicit rounds in each voice (manager/entrepreneur/visionary) so each is heard without dominance; surface dilemmas. *Artifact: dilemma list.*
6. **Design the pattern of renewal.** Actions per horizon: graceful H1 divestment + conservation; H2 portfolio with capture-protection for H2+; H3 seed nourishment (protect, connect, amplify the pockets). *Artifact: three-horizon action map.*
7. **Institutionalize sensing.** Assign ongoing scanning for H3 pockets and H1 decline signals (hand off to [horizon-scanning.md](horizon-scanning.md)). *Artifact: sensing routine.*

## Techniques, Tools & Deliverables

- The three-curve canvas (large wall chart; time on x-axis, system fitness/prevalence on y-axis).
- "Pockets of the future in the present" interviews/scanning as H3 evidence discipline.
- Three-voice role-play (manager / entrepreneur / visionary) to de-personalize conflict.
- Portfolio mapping: place live projects on H1/H2−/H2+/H3 to expose capture.
- **Deliverables:** annotated horizon map, conservation list, H2 portfolio with capture-risk ratings, action map, sensing routine.

## Strengths & Limitations

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Converts ideological conflict into positional dialogue | Qualitative; no built-in metrics or probabilities |
| Explicitly handles conservation (not just disruption) | Skilled facilitation matters; H1 voices usually dominate without it |
| Grounds the future in observable present pockets | Can be misread as a timeline ("H1 now, H3 later") rather than co-presence |
| Excellent bridge from foresight to innovation portfolios | H2+/H2− classification is judgment — contested and revisable |
| Fast: a useful map can emerge in one day | Direction-commitment: assumes transformation is needed at all |

In practice the method's most common failure is not misuse of the categories but capture: H1 incumbents dutifully tag their roadmap "H2" and the map hardens into a defense of the current portfolio. The timeline misreading ("H1 now, H3 later") is the second chronic failure — horizons co-exist, and a map that shows H3 as "the future, later" has reproduced the extrapolation thinking the method exists to break. Be candid about what the artifact is: a structured conversation, not evidence. It realigns a portfolio discussion; it does not validate any specific bet, and it says nothing about timing that the weak-signal work didn't already say.

## Worked Examples & Case Studies

- **Public service reform (UK):** used by futures/policy teams and organizations such as the UK's school-system reform community (Sharpe documents education cases) to structure transformation debates beyond "defend vs disrupt".
- **NHS and health-system futures work:** Three Horizons workshops have been used to map the shift from hospital-centric care (H1) toward community/preventive models (H3), identifying H2 innovations (telehealth, integrated care) and their capture risks.
- **IFF community practice:** the International Futures Forum has applied the method with governments, enterprises, and communities on sustainability transitions; documented in Sharpe (2013) and IFF resources.
- **Corporate portfolio reviews:** firms use the canvas to classify their project portfolio and discover that most "innovation" spend is H2− life support.

## Variants & Related Methodologies

- **Three Horizons for AI / sector-specific canvases** — the canvas re-themed per domain (IFF and others publish variants).
- **Combined with scenarios:** scenarios explore *which* H3 might come; Three Horizons structures *getting there*.
- **Combined with backcasting:** H3 vision + milestone pathway ([backcasting.md](backcasting.md)).

## Agent Adaptation

### Suitability for agent execution

**Medium.** The empirical legwork — finding H3 pockets of the future, gathering H1 decline evidence, inventorying H2 innovations — is strongly agent-suited (it is disciplined horizon scanning). The heart of the method, however, is a facilitated human conversation about values, identity, and letting go; agents can prepare and document it superbly but cannot *have* it for the participants. H2+/H2− classification can be drafted by agents but must be debated by the people whose projects are being classified.

### Recommended multi-agent workflow

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Scout | Find H3 pockets of the future (real projects, startups, practices, communities) and H1 decline signals (falling metrics, stranded assets, legitimacy crises) with sources. |
| Analyst | Structure findings into H1/H2/H3 inventories; draft H2+/H2− classifications with rationale and capture-risk notes. |
| Domain Expert (personas) | Interpret the map from sector perspectives (regulator, incumbent, insurgent, customer). |
| Critic / Red Team | Challenge: is this "H3 pocket" a real practice or a press release? Is the H1 decline claim sourced? Is the H2+ classification wishful? |
| Synthesizer | Assemble the annotated three-horizon map, conservation list, and action map drafts. |
| Verifier | Source-check every pocket and decline claim. |
| Facilitator (human) | Runs the three-voice conversation; owns conservation and portfolio decisions. |

### Agent pipeline

1. Frame (human) → domain definition.
2. Gather (Scout, two parallel tracks: "pockets of the future" / "decline signals") → `pockets.json`, `decline_signals.json` (each item sourced).
3. Analyze (Analyst) → H1/H2/H3 inventories + draft H2± classification → `horizon_map_draft.md`.
4. Stress-test (Critic + Verifier) → evidence-graded map (`confirmed | plausible | contested`).
5. Human workshop (Facilitator + participants) → the three-voice conversation; annotate, re-classify, decide.
6. Report (Synthesizer) → final map + conservation list + H2 portfolio actions + sensing routine (→ horizon scanning).

### Prompt templates

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Scout for a Three Horizons exercise on the domain "{{domain}}". Find
"pockets of the future in the present": REAL, currently operating practices, organizations,
pilots, or communities that embody a fundamentally different way of {{domain_function}} — not
concepts, proposals, or trend pieces. For each: name, location, what they actually do, why it is
H3 (what assumption of the current system it breaks), evidence of operation, source URLs.
Return 8-15 items as JSON. Reject anything you cannot source.
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Analyst. Domain: "{{domain}}". Here are proposed H2 innovations:
{{innovation_list}}. For each, classify H2+ (carries transformation toward the H3 themes:
{{h3_themes}}) or H2- (extends/legitimizes the current H1 system). Give a 2-sentence rationale,
a capture risk (who would have to NOT co-opt it for it to stay transformative), and what evidence
would change your classification. Output CSV.
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Critic. Here is a draft Three Horizons map: {{map}}. Attack it: Which H1
"decline" claims lack evidence? Which H3 pockets are marketing rather than practice? Where is
the map smuggling in the authors' ideology as if it were data? What H1 value is at risk of being
thrown away that belongs on the conservation list? Return the top 8 challenges, ranked.
```

### Tools & data requirements

Web search (pockets, decline evidence), retrieval over internal strategy/project-portfolio documents (for H2 inventory), canvas/diagram tooling for the map.

### Quality checks, failure modes & mitigations

| Failure mode | Detection | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| "Pockets" are press releases, not practices | Verifier cannot confirm operation | Hard rule: no unsourced pocket enters the map |
| Everything innovation-shaped gets labeled H2+ | Classification monotone | Critic must argue the H2− case for at least the top 3 by spend |
| H1 treated as pure villain | Conservation list empty | Facilitator gate: no workshop ends without a conservation list |
| Map read as a timeline | Participants say "phase 1/2/3" | Annotate co-presence explicitly on the canvas |
| Agent map pre-empts the human conversation | Participants feel "done already" | Agents deliver *drafts with contested flags*, never final maps |

### Human-in-the-loop checkpoints

1. Domain framing and the H3 vision themes (values).
2. The three-voice conversation itself (the method's core).
3. Conservation and portfolio decisions (money and identity).

### Inputs & outputs (chaining contract)

**Inputs:** domain definition; weak signals/pockets (from [horizon-scanning.md](horizon-scanning.md)); optionally a chosen scenario/vision as H3 (from [scenario-planning.md](scenario-planning.md)).
**Outputs:** annotated horizon map, H2+ portfolio (feeds [technology-roadmapping.md](technology-roadmapping.md) / [backcasting.md](backcasting.md)), sensing routine spec (feeds horizon scanning).
