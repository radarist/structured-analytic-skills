# Futures Triangle

> **Essence:** A rapid diagnostic that maps any issue's future as the contest of three forces — the **push** of the present (drivers), the **pull** of the future (images/visions), and the **weight** of the past (barriers/inertia) — revealing which futures are plausible and what is really holding change back.

## Overview

The futures triangle is Inayatullah's compact alternative to sprawling driver lists. For any issue, participants populate three corners of a triangle:

- **Push of the present** — quantitative drivers and trends pushing change forward: demographics, technology cost curves, regulation, market forces (the same material as [trend-analysis.md](trend-analysis.md) and [steep-pestle-analysis.md](steep-pestle-analysis.md)).
- **Pull of the future** — the images of the future that attract action: visions, goals, ideologies, iconic scenarios ("net zero 2050", "the smart city", "the paperless office"). Pulls exist because people act toward pictures of the future, not just from pushes.
- **Weight of the past** — what resists and constrains: sunk infrastructure, vested interests, culture, habits, laws, trauma, identity. Weights are usually under-analyzed in futures work, which is why naive change programs fail.

The analytic moves are simple and productive: (1) populate each corner with concrete items; (2) **size/weight the items** (which pushes are strongest? which pulls actually motivate anyone? which weights are heaviest?); (3) read the **plausible future** off the balance of forces — usually a compromise future where the strongest push meets the heaviest weight and the pull is weak or contested; (4) ask the strategic questions: what would *strengthen the pull* (a compelling shared image), *reduce the weights*, or *ride the pushes*? The triangle thus turns futures talk into a force-field strategy conversation (a descendant of Lewin's force-field analysis, with the future-image corner added).

It is fast (60–90 minutes), visual, works with any group, and pairs naturally with [causal-layered-analysis.md](causal-layered-analysis.md) (CLA explains *why* the weights weigh) and [backcasting.md](backcasting.md) (the pull corner, made operational).

## Origin & History

- Developed by **Sohail Inayatullah** in the 2000s as part of his "Six Pillars" futures-practice toolkit (Inayatullah, 2008, "Six Pillars: Futures Thinking for Transforming", *Foresight*, 10(1)); it has since become one of the most widely taught futures methods in government and university programs.
- Intellectual ancestry: Kurt Lewin's force-field analysis (1951); the futures-studies tradition of "images of the future" (Fred Polak, *The Image of the Future*, 1955/1973 — the classic on pulls); and Dator's emphasis on competing futures.
- Adopted in government foresight training (e.g., across Asia-Pacific futures programs, UNDP/UN futures work) and organizational strategy facilitation.

## Core Concepts & Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Push | A present driver of change with momentum (trend, driver, force). |
| Pull | An image of the future that attracts and organizes action (vision, goal, ideology, meme). |
| Weight | A barrier or inertial force from the past (structure, interest, culture, habit). |
| Weighting/sizing | Rating items by strength to find the balance of forces. |
| Plausible future | The future implied by the current balance of push/pull/weight. |
| Force-field analysis | Lewin's ancestor method (driving vs restraining forces). |
| Images of the future | Polak's thesis: societies/organizations are pulled by their dominant images. |

## When to Use / When Not to Use

**Use when:**
- Opening any futures engagement: a 90-minute shared map of the change forces on an issue.
- Diagnosing stalled change: the weights are usually the finding.
- Comparing stakeholder groups: each group's triangle reveals what they notice and ignore.
- As a front-end to scenarios, backcasting, or CLA.

**Don't use when:**
- You need quantified projections or deep causal models (it is a qualitative diagnostic).
- The group needs convergence on *one* future (the triangle maps forces, not choices).
- Used alone as "the foresight" — it is an opener/diagnostic, not a full methodology.
- Items are allowed to stay vague ("culture", "technology") — the method dies of abstraction unless items are concrete.

## Process & Steps

1. **State the issue and horizon.** One sentence, one target year. *Artifact: framing.*
2. **Populate PUSH.** 5–10 concrete drivers, each with evidence ("aging: median age 44→49 by 2040, national statistics"), not bare nouns. *Artifact: push list.*
3. **Populate PULL.** The images of the future operating on actors — official visions, competitor visions, cultural imaginaries. Note: *whose* pull? How motivating is it, really? *Artifact: pull list.*
4. **Populate WEIGHT.** Barriers: infrastructure, contracts, regulation, interests, habits, identity. Push for honesty — the weights people won't say aloud are the important ones. *Artifact: weight list.*
5. **Weight the items.** Rate each item's strength (e.g., 1–5, or dot-voting in workshops); visualize size on the triangle. *Artifact: weighted triangle.*
6. **Read the plausible future.** Given the balance: what future does this force field actually produce? Write it in 3 sentences. Is that the future anyone wants? *Artifact: plausible-future sketch.*
7. **Strategic questions.** What would strengthen the pull? Which weights can be reduced or converted? Which pushes can be ridden or accelerated? *Artifact: strategy question list + handoff to backcasting/scenarios.*

**Workshop parameters:** 4–25 people; 60–90 minutes; individuals/small groups populate first, then aggregate — aggregate-first anchoring shrinks the map.

## Techniques, Tools & Deliverables

- Triangle poster/canvas (three labeled corners; item size = strength).
- Dot-voting or 1–5 rating for weighting.
- Inter-item linkage marks (a weight that blocks a specific push; a pull that could dissolve a weight).
- Multiple-triangle comparison (per stakeholder group).
- **Deliverables:** weighted triangle map, plausible-future sketch, strategy-question list.

## Strengths & Limitations

| Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Extremely fast, intuitive, low-training | Qualitative; no rigor claims beyond structured dialogue |
| Restores barriers and vision to the driver conversation | Depth limited — each corner deserves its own method for real work |
| Excellent stakeholder-comparison tool | Weighting is judgment; can harden group opinion into "data" |
| Bridges naturally to scenarios, backcasting, CLA | Static snapshot; no dynamics of how forces evolve |
| Surfaces the unsaid (weights) with a legitimate format | Facilitator must enforce concreteness or it collapses into buzzwords |

Its weakness is the flip side of its speed: the triangle gives structure, not rigor. Without a facilitator who insists on concrete items ("aging population, 65+ share rising 0.3pp/yr" — not "demographics"), all three corners fill with nouns that mean nothing and the comparison value evaporates. It is also a static snapshot — the same forces six months later may draw a different triangle, and the artifact doesn't tell you that. Treat it as the opening diagnostic of a foresight engagement, not the analysis itself; each corner that matters deserves its own dedicated method (trend analysis for push, visioning/backcasting for pull, barrier analysis for weight).

## Worked Examples & Case Studies

- **Futures education worldwide:** the triangle is standard first-day practice in futures courses (Tamkang, Houston, Turku, Sunshine Coast) — e.g., triangles on "the future of the university" used to show students that pulls (rankings prestige) and weights (accreditation, identity) dominate pushes (online learning technology).
- **Government futures workshops:** used extensively in Asia-Pacific public-sector futures training (Inayatullah's work with multiple governments; documented in his "Six Pillars" and related papers).
- **Organizational strategy offsites:** common facilitator tool for change-diagnosis sessions; the weight corner routinely surfaces the real agenda (documented anecdotally across the futures-practice literature).

## Variants & Related Methodologies

- **Force-field analysis (Lewin)** — two-force ancestor (driving/restraining).
- **Multi-stakeholder triangle comparison** — one triangle per group, then contrast.
- **Triangle → scenario handoff:** the plausible future becomes the baseline scenario; pushes/weights seed driving forces.

## Agent Adaptation

### Suitability for agent execution

**High** for drafting and analysis; **the human group should still populate and weight its own triangle** where the purpose is organizational self-revelation. Agents excel at: evidence-backed push lists, honest weight inventories (agents have no face to lose — they can name the unspoken barriers, which is often the method's whole value), cross-group triangle comparison, and reading plausible futures off the weighted map. Use agents to prepare rich draft corners that humans then correct with lived knowledge — the corrections are data.

### Recommended multi-agent workflow

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Scout | Evidence for pushes (trend data) and pulls (existing visions, strategies, cultural imaginaries). |
| Analyst | Draft all three corners; propose item weightings with rationale; read the plausible future. |
| Domain Expert (personas of each stakeholder group) | Produce per-group triangles for comparison. |
| Critic / Red Team | Concreteness audit (kill bare nouns); honesty audit on weights (what's being avoided?); challenge the weightings. |
| Synthesizer | Triangle artifact, comparison report, strategy-question list. |
| Verifier | Source-check push/pull evidence. |
| Facilitator (human) | Owns framing, final weighting, and strategy decisions. |

### Agent pipeline

1. Frame (human) → issue + horizon.
2. Gather (Scout) → `pushes.json`, `pulls.json` (evidence-backed).
3. Analyze (Analyst + Domain Experts per stakeholder group) → draft weighted triangles → `triangles_draft.md`.
4. Stress-test (Critic) → concreteness/honesty audits → revisions.
5. Human workshop → correct and re-weight (corrections logged as findings).
6. Report (Synthesizer) → final map + plausible-future sketch + strategy questions → handoff.

### Prompt templates

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Analyst drafting the WEIGHT corner of a futures triangle on "{{issue}}"
(horizon {{horizon}}). List the barriers and inertial forces that will resist change, ordered by
strength. Include the ones polite analysis avoids: vested interests, identity investments, sunk
costs, regulatory lock-in, cultural habits, skill bases. For each: what exactly resists, whom it
belongs to, strength 1-5, and one piece of evidence or a named example. Be concrete — "culture"
is banned; say whose behavior, doing what, would have to change.
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Analyst. Here are the three populated corners: pushes {{pushes}}, pulls
{{pulls}}, weights {{weights}} (each item strength-rated). (1) Read the PLAUSIBLE FUTURE: given
this balance of forces, what actually happens by {{horizon}}? Three sentences, no hedging.
(2) Which single weight, if reduced, most changes the outcome? (3) Which pull is currently too
weak to matter but could plausibly be strengthened — and by whom? (4) What future does each
stakeholder group's triangle imply, and where do they disagree most?
```

```text
SYSTEM: You are the Critic. Futures triangle draft: {{triangle}}. (1) Concreteness audit: flag
every item that is a bare noun or buzzword; demand the specific version. (2) Honesty audit:
which obvious weights are missing (interests, identity, sunk cost)? (3) Evidence audit: which
pushes lack any data? (4) Balance audit: is one corner suspiciously thin? Return required fixes.
```

### Tools & data requirements

Web search (evidence), canvas/poster tooling for the artifact; nothing else required.

### Quality checks, failure modes & mitigations

| Failure mode | Detection | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Buzzword corners ("technology", "culture") | Critic concreteness audit | Ban bare nouns; require evidence or named example per item |
| Polite weights (real barriers unnamed) | Honesty audit | Agent drafts weights first (no face to lose); humans confirm |
| Weightings asserted without rationale | Missing rationale fields | Every rating carries a one-line justification |
| Triangle read as prediction | "So X will happen" talk | Frame output as force-field diagnosis → strategy questions |
| Agent map substitutes for group learning | Humans just approve | Workshop step where humans correct the draft is mandatory |

### Human-in-the-loop checkpoints

1. Issue framing and horizon.
2. Correcting/re-weighting the triangle (the corrections are the learning).
3. Strategy-question selection and handoff decisions.

### Inputs & outputs (chaining contract)

**Inputs:** issue + horizon; push evidence (from [horizon-scanning.md](horizon-scanning.md), [trend-analysis.md](trend-analysis.md), [steep-pestle-analysis.md](steep-pestle-analysis.md)); existing visions/pulls from strategy documents.
